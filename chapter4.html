Correct Answer

The servers can't talk to each other. Now you have two options:

Option A: Both servers sell the ticket. Two fans show up at the venue. One has no seat. You prioritized availability.

Option B: Both servers freeze sales until the network heals. Nobody can buy. You prioritized consistency.

There is no Option C. This is the CAP Theorem — and every distributed system in the world faces this choice.

C, A, and P

Three guarantees. You can only have two. Here's what each means.

C

Consistency

Every read returns the most recent write. All nodes see the same data at the same time. No stale reads, ever.

A

Availability

Every request gets a response (not an error). The system never says "try again later" — even if data might be stale.

P

Partition Tolerance

The system works even when network messages between nodes are dropped or delayed. Nodes can't communicate.

!

The Catch

In any distributed system, partitions WILL happen. So you're really choosing between CP and AP. CA is a fantasy in production.

Analogy: Imagine two librarians in different buildings managing the same book catalog. A storm knocks out the phone line between them (partition). Now: do they stop lending books until the line is fixed (consistency)? Or keep lending and risk giving out the same book twice (availability)?

Google's Chubby team measured their distributed lock service and found: network partitions caused 7.6% of all outages. Not disk. Not CPU. The network.

Key insight: You don't choose whether partitions happen. You choose what your system does when they happen. That's the entire CAP theorem in one sentence.

CP vs AP: The Real Choice

Consistent and down, or available and wrong?
🔒

CP — Consistency + Partition Tolerance

"I'd rather be down than wrong."

  • Behavior: During a partition, reject requests or wait until sync
  • Tradeoff: Some requests get errors or timeouts
  • Best for: Financial transactions, inventory, booking systems
Examples: MongoDB (default) · HBase · Redis Cluster · Zookeeper
OR
🌱

AP — Availability + Partition Tolerance

"I'd rather be wrong than down."

  • Behavior: During a partition, serve requests with potentially stale data
  • Tradeoff: Nodes may return different answers temporarily
  • Best for: Social feeds, DNS, shopping carts, content delivery
Examples: Cassandra · DynamoDB · CouchDB · Riak

Back to our concert: A CP system would show "Service Unavailable" to both fans until servers reconnect. Nobody double-sells, but millions see errors. An AP system would let both buy. When the network heals, one gets a refund — with priority access to the next show.

The business question: What costs more — a double-sold ticket you can fix in 5 minutes, or a crashed app when 2 million fans are refreshing? Ticketmaster picks AP. Banks pick CP. Neither is wrong.

The System Design Interview

"Design a ticket booking system like Ticketmaster." Here's how to nail it.
"Two fans buy the last Taylor Swift ticket at the exact same second. What happens?"
✘ THE REJECTED ANSWER
"I'll lock the ticket row in the database. First transaction wins. Second user gets 'Sold Out.' During a network partition, both servers wait until they can sync. No purchase goes through until consistency is guaranteed."
Interviewer: "So during a network blip on sale day, with 2 million fans refreshing... your system just freezes and Ticketmaster trends on Twitter again?"
Same question. Better answer.
✔ THE STAFF-LEVEL ANSWER
"It depends on the business cost. For concert tickets: accept both purchases (AP), resolve the conflict async. One fan gets an instant refund + priority queue for next release. On sale day, a frozen app loses more revenue than an oversell you fix in 3 minutes. But for a bank transfer? I'd pick CP — showing an error is better than transferring money twice."
The CAP Decision Framework
1
Can users tolerate stale data?
Yes = lean AP. No = lean CP.
↓
2
What's the cost of being wrong?
Financial loss = CP. Bad UX = AP.
↓
3
Can conflicts be resolved after the fact?
Mergeable = AP. Not mergeable = CP.
↓
CP
Choose CP when: money moves, inventory is critical, or conflicts can't be undone.
AP
Choose AP when: stale data is tolerable, conflicts are resolvable, or downtime kills revenue.

The golden phrase: "What's more expensive — being wrong for 30 seconds, or being down for 30 seconds?" That one question in an interview shows you understand the business impact of CAP, not just the theory.

Key Takeaways

What to remember when CAP comes up in interviews or architecture reviews
⚡

Partitions Are Guaranteed

You don't choose P. You choose what happens when P occurs: sacrifice C or sacrifice A.

💰

Think Business, Not Theory

"What's the cost of being wrong vs being down?" is the only question that matters.

🔄

CAP Is a Spectrum

Modern systems are tunable. Cassandra does per-query consistency. It's not a binary choice.

💡

Design for Recovery

The best systems don't just pick CP or AP — they design how to recover when things go wrong.
